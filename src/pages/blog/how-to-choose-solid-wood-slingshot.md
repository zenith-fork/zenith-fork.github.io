Choose a solid-wood slingshot by its grip profile, finished dimensions, sound grain, and band attachment method. Compare the exact frame pictured rather than relying on a pattern name or wood species. For an 80 mm frame, check tip width and overall height too: the Flying Tiger and Large Mantis share that outer width but have different proportions.

The grain is usually what makes someone stop scrolling. That is a good reason to look closer, but I would leave the final choice until the measurements and side views make sense.

A handsome frame can still be too full through the palm, too narrow at the waist, or fitted with an attachment system you do not enjoy maintaining. Those details become more noticeable after the first few minutes in the hand. This guide starts with them.

## Decide what you want the handle to feel like

Some people like a narrow waist that gives the thumb and fingers a clear place to settle. Others prefer a rounded handle that fills more of the palm. Neither preference follows directly from hand size. Two people with similar hands can want very different shapes.

If you already own a comfortable frame, use it as a reference. Measure its height and width, then look at where the handle narrows and where it becomes fullest. A familiar object tells you more than a generic “large hand” label.

For a pinch hold, examine the area immediately beneath the forks. For thumb support, look at the shoulder where the thumb will rest. The side view matters here: a front photograph cannot show how far the handle projects into the palm. Our [pinch grip and thumb-support comparison](/blog/pinch-grip-vs-thumb-support-wooden-slingshot/) explains those differences in more detail.

## Choose how you want to change bands

Attachment style is easy to overlook while comparing wood grain. It is also something you will handle repeatedly.

If convenient band replacement is a priority, examine the [Desert Ironwood Flying Tiger quick-clamp frame](/products/fork-desert-ironwood-flying-tiger/). Its brass screw-down caps hold the latex at the tips. Correct positioning and even tightening still matter; a clamp does not remove the need to inspect the bands.

If you prefer an all-wood frame without fitted metal hardware, the [Crimson Hardwood Sculpted U](/products/fork-crimson-hardwood-classic-80/) uses traditional wrap-and-tuck tips. It is listed at 80 mm outer width and 120 mm height. The tying strip is replaceable, and the smooth tip surfaces are exposed for inspection whenever the bands are removed.

The round-tip Flying Tiger and Large Mantis listings include brass tip plates. If the precise attachment procedure matters to your choice, ask for a close-up and confirmation before ordering. A plate visible in a photo should not be assumed to work exactly like a screw-down cap.

For a broader comparison, read [quick-clamp versus wrap-and-tuck attachment](/blog/quick-clamp-vs-wrap-and-tuck-slingshot-bands/).

## Treat wood species as context

Density affects weight and how much volume a handle can carry comfortably. Grain direction and the condition of the individual blank matter to the finished structure. Colour and figure affect appearance. These are related considerations, but they are not interchangeable.

An expensive timber does not guarantee a better fit. A dark streak is not proof of a particular species. On our two red-brown solid-wood frames, the material is described generally rather than assigned an unconfirmed exotic name.

Look for clear photographs of both faces, the fork roots, and the sides. Ask about any line you cannot distinguish from a check. The [wood selection guide](/blog/choosing-wood-for-handmade-slingshot/) covers what to examine beyond the species label.

## Keep replacement bands in the decision

Before buying, confirm the intended band orientation, attachment compatibility, and what is included. Frame price does not tell you whether a fitted bandset or spare bands are supplied.

The [tapered replacement bandsets](/products/bands-pro-latex-pack-10/) are a separate listing. Tell us your frame and measured draw length when asking about suitability. Tip width alone is not enough to choose the working length or the complete setup.

## Questions worth asking before payment

### Will I receive the exact wooden slingshot pictured?

Our one-of-one frames are offered as the exact piece shown. Confirm that the listing is still available before paying. Grain patterns and hand-shaped details cannot be reproduced exactly in another frame.

### Which is better, Flying Tiger or Mantis?

Choose by proportions and preferred grip. The round-tip Flying Tiger is shorter with narrower tips and a sculpted waist. The Large Mantis is taller with broader shoulders and a fuller palm swell. Neither is a universal upgrade over the other.

### Is an 80 mm frame suitable for me?

Outer width gives a useful starting point, but it does not settle fit. Compare height, tip width, handle thickness, and side profile with something comfortable in your hand. Ask for a missing measurement rather than estimating it from perspective.

## A practical way to narrow the choice

Start with the handle you prefer. Compare the measurements. Decide how you want to change bands. Then choose the particular grain and finish that appeal to you.

That order leaves room to enjoy the wood without asking appearance to answer questions about fit. Browse the [current handmade wooden slingshot collection](/#forks), open the individual galleries, and ask about anything the photographs leave unresolved.
