Amber tying tape is a narrow, flexible strip used to secure slingshot flat bands at suitable attachment points. It is binding material, not the working band that stores energy during the draw. Zenith Fork Studio offers it in 20 m rolls at USD 12.80 per roll; confirm compatibility with your frame and band attachment before ordering.

The name can be confusing in an English product listing. “Amber band” sounds as though it describes the colour of a shooting bandset. In this case, it refers to the smaller tying material wound onto a spool. It has a different job from the two broad strips running between the frame and pouch.

That distinction is worth making before comparing prices. A roll of binding material and a pack of ready-made flat bands are separate workshop supplies.

## What the tying tape actually holds

In a traditional wrap-and-tuck attachment, a narrow strip wraps around the fork tip and the main flat band. The final end is tucked beneath an earlier turn to retain the wrap. The main band needs to remain aligned and supported against a smooth contact surface.

The tying material provides the binding. It does not correct a chipped fork tip, an uneven edge, or a band placed in the wrong direction. Those details need attention before wrapping starts.

A spool lets you cut material as needed rather than keeping a collection of short lengths. Keep the loose end accessible and protect the roll from workshop dust. A strip that has picked up abrasive particles should not be wrapped against clean latex.

## Tying tape and flat bands are not interchangeable

The working flat bands are selected around the complete setup, including latex type, thickness, taper, active length, pouch, and the user's draw length. Binding tape is selected for its attachment role.

Buying a long roll of tying material does not supply replacement shooting bands. If you need the broad strips and pouch as an assembly, look at the separate [tapered flat bandsets](/products/bands-pro-latex-pack-10/).

Likewise, do not assume that any elastic strip can replace a tying material. Width, thickness, surface grip, and condition affect how a wrap sits. Use material intended for the attachment and ask about compatibility when the dimensions are not supplied.

Send the frame name or a close photograph of the tip, and explain what you intend to attach. A question such as “Will this suit the traditional tips on this frame?” gives us something specific to check. “Will it work on every slingshot?” does not: attachment surfaces and hardware vary too much.

For a wooden frame, inspect the contact area first. Smooth, rounded edges matter because the main band is repeatedly stretched near that point. More turns of tape cannot make an unsuitable surface acceptable.

## Keep the roll clean between band changes

Store tying material without tension and away from direct light, prolonged heat, oils, and household cleaning products. A clean closed bag or drawer helps keep grit off the strip. Keep it separate from used ties and workshop offcuts so damaged material does not find its way back into a new attachment.

There is no fixed replacement interval stated for this product. Inspect it when cutting a new length. Discard sections with cuts, cracking, contamination, or an unexplained change in feel rather than building a fresh attachment around questionable material.
