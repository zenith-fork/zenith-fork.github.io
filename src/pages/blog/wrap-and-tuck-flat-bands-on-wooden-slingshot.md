Wrap-and-tuck is the traditional way to attach flat bands to a slingshot frame without a screw-down clamp. A narrow tying strip is stretched around the fork tip, the final turn is tucked beneath an earlier wrap, and friction keeps the assembly in place.

The method is simple, compact, and well suited to an all-wood frame. It is also easy to do carelessly. Secure attachment begins with the condition of the fork tips and the alignment of the bands, not with pulling the tying strip as hard as possible.

## Inspect the fork tips first

Remove the old attachment completely and clean away loose debris. The wood should be smooth, the edge radius should be even, and there should be no chip, sharp corner, or raised finish where the latex will sit.

Run a soft cloth over the contact area. If fibres catch, find out why before fitting a new band. Do not use the new latex as a test strip; a tiny defect can mark its edge before the first draw.

On a handmade wooden slingshot, the two tips may be shaped individually while still presenting matching attachment surfaces. Visual symmetry is useful, but smooth support and consistent band position matter more than making every curve mathematically identical.

## Decide on over-the-top or through-the-fork orientation

Flat bands may leave the tips over the top or through the fork gap, depending on frame geometry and user preference. The important point is that the chosen orientation suits the tip shape and is used consistently on both sides.

Do not force a band around an edge that was not prepared for that path. If the frame was sold with a recommended orientation, follow it. When uncertain, ask the maker and include a clear photograph of the tips.

Our article on [quick-clamp versus wrap-and-tuck bands](/blog/quick-clamp-vs-wrap-and-tuck-slingshot-bands/) compares the two attachment systems in more detail.

## Align both bands before wrapping

Place equal lengths of latex on the contact areas and check that the tapers face the same direction. The edges should lie flat rather than curl or cross. A small removable mark on the band or a simple measuring card can help position both sides consistently.

Uneven attachment changes the active length. Even if the finished wraps look tidy, one side beginning several millimetres farther forward will not match the other.

Take time at this stage. It is easier to correct alignment before the first turn than after a tight wrap has been completed.

## Use a suitable tying strip

The tying material needs enough elasticity and grip to hold its tension without cutting into the main band. Narrow latex strips are commonly used. Whatever material is chosen, it should be clean, undamaged, and compatible with the band.

Start with controlled tension and overlap the turns neatly. Each wrap should support the previous one without forming a hard ridge. Excessive stretching does not compensate for poor placement; it can damage the tying strip, deform the main band, or make removal unnecessarily difficult.

The final tuck should sit securely beneath the earlier turns. Leave enough tail to inspect and remove the attachment later, but not so much that it interferes with the band path.

## Check the finished attachment without a full draw

Compare the exposed active lengths and confirm that both bands leave the tips along matching paths. Look for folds, trapped debris, damaged edges, or a tying strip sitting too close to a sharp transition.

Apply only light, controlled tension at first while keeping the setup pointed in a safe direction. Stop if a wrap shifts or the latex begins to roll. Correct the attachment rather than adding another layer over a problem.

After the first short session, inspect both wraps again. Latex settles under load, and a neat attachment can reveal movement that was not visible on the bench.

## Removal is part of good attachment practice

When changing bands, do not cut toward the wooden tip. Unwind the strip or lift it carefully with a blunt tool that cannot score the finish. A small knife mark across the attachment area can become a latex-damaging edge.

Once removed, inspect the wood before installing the next set. This repeated check is one advantage of a traditional system: the load-bearing area is exposed every time the bands are changed.

Wrap-and-tuck attachment works because the surfaces, alignment, material, and tension cooperate. A harder pull on the tying strip cannot replace any of those details.

Inspect the frame and bands before use, wear eye protection, use a secure backstop, and follow all applicable laws.
