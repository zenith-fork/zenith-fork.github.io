Our Flying Tiger round-tip, Large Mantis, and Golden-Grain frames all have an 80 mm outer fork width, but their handles and tip dimensions differ. Flying Tiger is 120 mm high with 14 mm tips; Large Mantis is 130 mm high with 17 mm tips; Golden-Grain is approximately 130 mm high with 15 mm tips. Compare the grip profiles and missing measurements before choosing by width alone.

The outside span is often the easiest number to remember. It is also the number most likely to make three different frames sound interchangeable. Put the galleries beside one another and that impression disappears: the waist, shoulders, and lower grip each use the available space differently.

This article compares the three pictured solid-wood pieces currently listed by Zenith Fork Studio. The Flying Tiger here is the round-tip model, rather than the separate Desert Ironwood quick-clamp listing.

## Flying Tiger: the shorter reference

The [Flying Tiger round-tip frame](/products/fork-flying-tiger-solid-wood-round-tip-80/) has a pronounced waist beneath a deep U-shaped opening. At 120 mm overall height, it is the shortest of these three listings.

Consider its gallery first if you prefer a defined narrow area for your fingers and less handle length below the shoulders. That is a comparison of shapes, not a claim that it fits every smaller hand. Thickness and the position of the palm contact are still important.

The 14 mm tip width is also the narrowest here. It does not independently specify a suitable bandset or determine the inner fork gap. Those details should be confirmed rather than calculated from the outside span alone.

## Large Mantis: fuller shoulders and palm contact

The [Large Mantis](/products/fork-large-mantis-solid-wood-80/) carries broader shoulders into a rounded palm swell. Its 17 mm tips and 130 mm height distinguish it from the Flying Tiger even before the side profile is considered.

It is a useful candidate for someone who finds slender handles underfilled. Study where the swell begins and which face projects into the palm. More wood in the handle may feel reassuring to one person and intrusive to another.

The higher listed price is not a measured improvement in accuracy or speed. It describes a different handmade object. Buy its shape because the contact surfaces make sense to you, rather than treating the price as a performance ranking.

## Golden-Grain: a defined waist and lower flare

The [Golden-Grain sculpted frame](/products/fork-golden-grain-solid-wood-80/) combines 15 mm tips with a deeply shaped handle and an outward-flaring base. The height is close to the Mantis, but the transitions through the waist and lower grip give it a separate character.

The golden-brown colour and darker grain are appearance descriptions. The wood is confirmed as solid, but the species has not been confirmed. Choose it on the basis of the listed facts and actual-item photographs rather than assigning a timber name from colour.

## Ask the same questions about all three

Request handle depth at the waist and palm swell if you need a closer fit comparison. Ask for bare-frame weight if weight matters. Confirm the intended holding hand, especially where the shaping is asymmetric.

All three listings describe metal tip plates. Do not assume that every plate is a screw-down clamp or that matching materials imply matching installation methods. Confirm how the bands should be attached to the exact piece.

Our [measurement checklist](/blog/wooden-slingshot-measurements-checklist/) provides a consistent way to request missing dimensions. Keeping the questions the same makes the answers easier to compare.

## Use a familiar grip as your reference

If you own a comfortable frame, compare its waist, shoulder position, lower handle, and side depth with these pieces. A reference you have held is more useful than a general hand-size label.

If you have no reference frame, use the side photographs and ask the workshop about the intended contact surfaces. State which hand holds the frame and which draws the pouch. Our [handedness guide](/blog/left-or-right-handed-wooden-slingshot/) explains why that wording is clearer than simply saying you are right-handed.

The shortest route to a useful choice is to narrow the shapes first, then confirm the missing facts. Flying Tiger provides the shorter outline, Mantis the broader palm-filling profile, and Golden-Grain a pronounced waist with a flared base. The shared 80 mm measurement is where the comparison begins, not where it ends.

Inspect the frame and bands before use, wear eye protection, use a secure backstop, and follow local law.
