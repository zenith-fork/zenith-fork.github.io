An 85 mm wooden slingshot is 5 mm wider across the outside of its fork tips than an 80 mm frame. That difference does not automatically mean a wider inner gap, a larger handle, or better performance. Tip width and the full frame profile must be compared as well. Our 85 mm solid-wood frame has 18 mm tips; the 80 mm Flying Tiger has 14 mm tips, and the 80 mm Large Mantis has 17 mm tips.

Five millimetres sounds like a small change. On a handheld object, it is visible, but it still tells only part of the story. Two frames can gain width in quite different places: one spreads the tips farther apart, while another carries more wood in each tip.

If you are choosing between these sizes online, start with the measurements rather than treating 85 mm as the next step up from 80 mm.

The missing height on the 85 mm listing is worth noticing. It should be measured and confirmed if overall scale matters to your choice. A photograph can show proportions without supplying a reliable absolute measurement.

## Outer width is not the same as clear space

Imagine keeping the outer width fixed and making both tips wider. More material would extend into the opening, leaving less clear space between them. That is why outside width alone cannot tell you the fork gap.

It is tempting to calculate the gap by subtracting both tip widths from the outer width. On a simple drawing with straight, parallel measurements, that can describe the geometry. On a carved frame, the tip faces may be rounded or angled, and the widest outside points may not lie on the same measurement line as the inside edges.

Ask for the actual clear gap if it is important to you. Do not convert approximate catalogue measurements into a precise gap that has never been measured.

## Wider tips do not prescribe stronger bands

An 18 mm tip describes a surface dimension. It does not specify the latex thickness, taper, active length, or draw demand that should be used. Nor does it establish the intended attachment method.

Confirm how the bands should sit on the exact frame. Rounded wooden tips and fitted brass plates should not be treated as identical systems simply because their widths are close. Ask for an attachment close-up if the gallery does not resolve the question.

Band selection belongs to the complete setup and the user's measured draw length. More space at the tip is not an instruction to fit the widest or heaviest band available.

## Which width should you choose?

If you already own a comfortable frame, compare its outside width, tip width, overall height, and side profile with the candidate. Keep the measurements separate in your notes. A familiar handle can tell you whether a fuller palm swell is desirable; outside width cannot answer that alone.

If you have no reference frame, ask for the missing dimensions and intended holding hand. Look at both faces and both sides before deciding. With asymmetric woodwork, a curve facing the wrong direction can matter more than a 5 mm width difference.

The [solid-wood slingshot buying guide](/blog/how-to-choose-solid-wood-slingshot/) brings grip, material, and attachment style into the same decision. Choose the piece whose proportions you can understand, then confirm availability and order details by email.

Inspect the frame and bands before use, wear eye protection, use a secure backstop, and follow local law.
