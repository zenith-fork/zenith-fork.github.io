When a handmade wooden slingshot arrives, first compare it with the confirmed listing and inspect it under good light without drawing the bands. Check the wood around the fork roots and tips, the handle, fitted hardware, and any supplied accessories. Photograph unexpected marks or damage and contact the seller before using a questionable frame.

It is tempting to fit bands as soon as the parcel is open. A few quiet minutes at the table are more useful. You can check the exact piece, see how its finish looks in ordinary light, and notice details that packaging or studio photographs may have hidden.

This is an inspection guide, not a statement of return terms. Order contents and any arrangements about shipping or after-sales support should be confirmed in your email correspondence.

## Keep the packaging while you check the contents

Open the parcel carefully and avoid cutting toward the frame. Set the wrapping aside until you have checked the item and everything agreed with the seller. Photograph visible transit damage before discarding damaged packaging.

If several items were ordered, compare them with the written confirmation. A frame, spare bandsets, and tying tape are separate products unless they were explicitly included together. A display stand in a gallery photograph is not automatically part of the order.

For our [amber tying tape](/products/bands-amber-tying-tape-20m/), the listed unit is one 20 m roll even though some images show several rolls. Reading the unit description before and after delivery avoids an unnecessary mismatch in expectations.

## Match the frame with the actual-item photographs

On a one-of-one wooden frame, grain lines are useful reference points. Compare the front, reverse, and sides with the product gallery. Follow a few distinctive lines rather than expecting the colour to match your screen exactly.

Warm indoor lighting can make red-brown wood look more orange. Daylight from a window may bring out different contrast. Give the piece a look in neutral, indirect light before treating a colour difference as evidence of a different item.

## Inspect the wood without trying to stress-test it

Rotate the frame beneath a bright light. Look around the fork roots, both tips, the narrow waist, and any hardware holes. Check for an open line, chipped edge, loose fragment, or a mark that was not shown or discussed before purchase.

Do not bend the forks to see whether a questionable line opens. A home stress test can worsen a defect and still fail to establish whether the piece is sound. If you are unsure, stop and send a clear photograph instead.

Natural pores and dark grain lines are not automatically cracks. Equally, an attractive surface should not persuade you to ignore a new opening or movement. Good communication begins with a close-up and a plain description of what you see.

## Check the surfaces that touch the bands

On traditional tips, the contact surfaces should be smooth and the edges appropriately rounded. A small raised splinter or sharp finish ridge deserves attention before latex is fitted.

On frames with hardware, check that components appear seated and that nothing moves unexpectedly. Do not compensate for uncertainty by tightening every fastener as hard as possible. Ask for the intended fitting procedure if it was not supplied.

The [quick-clamp and wrap-and-tuck comparison](/blog/quick-clamp-vs-wrap-and-tuck-slingshot-bands/) explains why different systems need different checks. A brass plate visible on a tip should not be assumed to function as a screw-down clamp.

## Handle it gently before assembling the setup

With no bands under tension, hold the frame in the intended hand and let your fingers settle. Compare the contact areas with the product description. Notice whether the palm swell and thumb support face the expected direction.

A grip can feel unfamiliar at first, but it should not require forcing the wrist around a pronounced contour. If handedness appears wrong, clarify it before altering the wood. Sanding away a pressure point can change the shape and finish while making the original problem harder to assess.

## Inspect supplied elastic parts separately

If bands were included, check the latex edges, pouch, ties, and attachment areas. Do not assume that being new removes the need for inspection. Look for nicks, contamination, or other visible damage before fitting them.

Confirm the intended active length and compatibility rather than fitting an unfamiliar bandset simply because it arrived in the same parcel. Frame dimensions alone do not establish a suitable setup for your draw length.

If you are storing spares, keep them relaxed and away from heat and direct light. Our [latex storage guide](/blog/how-to-store-latex-slingshot-bands/) covers that routine.

## Send useful information if something is wrong

Email the product name, order correspondence, a full view, and a close-up of the concern. Use ordinary light and keep the original files. Include packaging photographs if the parcel was visibly damaged.

Avoid applying oil, sanding, or attempting a repair before discussing an unexpected defect. Those changes can hide evidence and make it harder to understand what happened.

Once the piece has been checked, routine care is straightforward: keep it dry, wipe it clean, and inspect it before each session. See [wooden slingshot care](/blog/how-to-care-for-a-wooden-slingshot/) for cleaning and finish maintenance. Wear eye protection, use a secure backstop, and follow local law whenever using the completed setup.
